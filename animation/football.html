FIFA World Cup

Greatest moments · 8 photos

The greatest moments in FIFA World Cup history span decades of unmatched drama, featuring Pele’s majestic 1970 header, Maradona’s polarizing "Hand of God," and Messi’s triumphant 2022 finale. These legendary milestones continue to define the pinnacle of international football.

Decades of Drama
1970
Pele’s Header and the "Game of the Century"
Brazil’s spectacular 4-1 victory over Italy in the 1970 final featured Pele’s 100th World Cup goal. He soared in the air, hanging long enough to head it past the keeper. This tournament is also home to the legendary 1970 semi-final between Italy and West Germany, a 4-3 thriller often called the "Game of the Century". YouTube·FIFA +1
1986
Maradona’s Brilliance and Controversy
Diego Maradona cemented his legacy in Mexico in two unforgettable ways during a single match against England. First came the notorious "Hand of God" goal, followed shortly by the "Goal of the Century," where he dribbled past five English players from his own half to score.
1998
Ronaldo’s Mystery and Zidane’s Triumph
The 1998 final in Paris saw French icon Zinedine Zidane score two headers to lead France to a 3-0 victory over Brazil, capturing their first-ever World Cup on home soil. This tournament is also famously remembered for the mysterious medical episode suffered by Brazilian star Ronaldo just hours before kickoff.
2010
Iniesta and the African Stage
The 2010 World Cup in South Africa marked the first time the tournament was hosted on the continent. The iconic opening goal by Siphiwe Tshabalala against Mexico remains a massive fan favorite. The tournament culminated in Andres Iniesta's dramatic extra-time winner, giving Spain its first World Cup title against the Netherlands.
2022
Messi and the Ultimate Final
The 2022 tournament in Qatar delivered what many consider the greatest World Cup final in history. Lionel Messi led Argentina to glory, but not before an epic back-and-forth battle with Kylian Mbappé, who scored a hat-trick for France. The match ultimately went to a penalty shootout, resulting in Argentina’s first trophy since 1986. FIFA
